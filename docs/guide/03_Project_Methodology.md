# Project Methodology

## Phase 1 — Theory
1. Explain Prisoner's Dilemma.
2. Present payoff matrix.
3. Find dominant strategies.
4. Find Nash equilibrium.
5. Explain Pareto inferiority.

## Phase 2 — Human experiment
Suggested participants: 20 students (adjust to your class).
- Pair participants.
- Play 10 rounds.
- Each round: secretly choose C or D.
- Reveal simultaneously.
- Record choices and payoffs.

Data fields:
Student/Pair, Round, Player A choice, Player B choice, A payoff, B payoff.

## Phase 3 — Computer tournament
Use Python.
Suggested strategies:
1. Always Cooperate
2. Always Defect
3. Tit-for-Tat
4. Grim Trigger
5. Tit-for-Two-Tats
6. Random

Run round-robin matches.
Suggested: 100 rounds per match.
Record total score, average score, cooperation rate, and defection rate.

## Phase 4 — Compare
Compare:
- theoretical prediction
- human cooperation
- computer strategy performance

## Optional trust survey
Before the game:
"How likely do you think your opponent is to cooperate?" (1–5)
After the game:
"How much did you trust your opponent?" (1–5)
Compare with actual cooperation.
