# Theory Study Guide

## 1. Players, strategies, actions, outcomes, payoffs
- Players: decision makers.
- Strategies: rules/plans for choosing actions.
- Actions: Cooperate (C) or Defect (D).
- Payoffs: points received after both players choose.

## 2. Dominant strategy
A strategy is dominant if it gives a player a better payoff regardless of what the opponent does.
For the payoff matrix:
- If B chooses C: A gets 3 with C, 5 with D.
- If B chooses D: A gets 0 with C, 1 with D.
Therefore D is dominant for A. Symmetrically, D is dominant for B.

## 3. Nash equilibrium
A Nash equilibrium is an outcome where no player can improve their payoff by changing strategy alone.
(D,D) is the unique pure-strategy Nash equilibrium for the classic matrix.

## 4. Pareto comparison
(C,C) = (3,3) is better for both players than (D,D) = (1,1).
Therefore (D,D) is Pareto-inferior to (C,C).

## 5. The dilemma
Individual rationality points toward D, while collective benefit is higher at C,C.

## 6. Repeated games
When the game is repeated, a player's current choice can affect future responses.
The textbook discusses the finite-horizon backward-induction problem and the possibility of cooperation when continuation is uncertain.

## 7. Tit-for-Tat
- Cooperate on the first move.
- Then copy the opponent's previous move.
Important properties discussed in the book:
Nice, retaliatory, forgiving, and clear.

## 8. Trust and social psychology
A classroom experiment can measure expected cooperation/trust before play and compare it with actual behavior.
Do not claim causation from a small classroom experiment.
