# Textbook Reference Notes

Primary source:
Philip D. Straffin, Game Theory and Strategy.

Relevant chapters:
- Chapter 11 — Nash Equilibria
- Chapter 12 — The Prisoner's Dilemma
- Chapter 13 — Application to Social Psychology

Important textbook-supported ideas:
- Prisoner's Dilemma has a dominant defection strategy in the classic formulation.
- Mutual defection is a Nash equilibrium but is Pareto-inferior to mutual cooperation.
- The general PD conditions are T > R > U > S and R > (S+T)/2.
- Repeated play introduces the shadow/future interaction issue.
- The textbook discusses finite repeated play and the backward-induction/domino argument.
- Uncertain continuation can create a theoretical possibility for cooperation.
- Axelrod-style results emphasize Tit-for-Tat.
- Successful strategies are associated with being nice, retaliatory, forgiving, and clear.
- Chapter 13 connects Prisoner's Dilemma to trust/suspicion and experimental social psychology.
